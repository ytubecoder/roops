01 · What it is · 何

Small agents, on a schedule, that only ever report.

Roops is a thin harness for recurring agent jobs — the boring, valuable kind: audits, drift checks, health sweeps. Each loop wakes on its schedule, looks at one thing carefully, files its findings, and disappears.

一

Rounds, not sessions

Every firing is a fresh agent with no memory of the last run. State lives in the harness — run history, findings, dispositions — never in the model. The patrol is the same every night; only the garden changes.

二

Rounds are read-only — but findings are actions in waiting

While on patrol, a loop can only look and speak. That is enforced beneath the model — write sandbox, tool restrictions, no network — not promised in a prompt. But don’t mistake the patrol for the product: findings are written as fully-specified actions, precise enough to execute as-is. Nothing changes during a round; things change because of one.

三

Your seal moves the world

Findings flow to one place — roops stamp. Acknowledge, dismiss, snooze only quiet the garden. Approval, 承, is different: an approved finding becomes an order, executed on its own audited path — dry-run, execute, confirm — never mid-patrol. Approve the same thing often enough and Roops asks to make the approval standing. Autonomy is earned, one seal at a time.

四

Engine-neutral

Prompts are written for no particular model. Engines are switchable per loop — one CLI-driven agent today, a different one tomorrow, a local model later. The harness outlives any engine it runs.

02 · Three pillars · 三柱

Minimal as a garden.
Dependable as a guard.
Arranged as a vase.

枯山水karesansui · dry garden

The garden is raked every morning

A zen garden is not built once — it is tended on a schedule. The raking is the product. Roops is the same object: recurring, disciplined maintenance whose output is order, not novelty. And like a garden, its default state is silence — the aesthetic principle of ma (間), meaningful empty space. A quiet dashboard is not “no data”; it is the deliberate, beautiful display of nothing being wrong.

武士道bushidō · the way

The rounds are kept. The blade stays sheathed.

A castle guard walks the same perimeter at the same hour, every night, whether or not anything happened the night before. That patrol is junkai (巡回) — and it is exactly what a scheduled run is. The second half of the discipline matters just as much: the patrol itself is read-only, enforced at the engine level. On rounds, the guard observes and reports. The acting comes after — when the lord seals an order, it is carried out in daylight, on its own path, with its own record. The blade is drawn on command, never on patrol.

maps to → cron-like reliability · per-loop permission axes · rounds propose · 承 approves · execution on its own audited path
生け花ikebana · living flowers

Findings are arranged, not dumped

Classical ikebana arranges three stems at three heights: shin (真), the tallest; soe (副), the middle; hikae (控), the lowest. This is — exactly, with no forcing — a severity hierarchy: shin = alert, soe = warn, hikae = info. A run’s findings are a small arrangement: few stems, deliberate placement, and the cutting away of everything that does not belong. Dismissal is part of the art.

03 · Working vocabulary · 語彙

Words the product borrows.

Not decoration — each term earns its place by naming something the harness already does. Used sparingly: one or two in the UI, the rest in docs and voice.

巡meguru / junto go around; a patrolA single firing of a loop. “Last 巡: 06:00.” CLI verb: roops rounds.
手入れteiretending, upkeepThe category of work Roops does — garden-keeping for repos and systems. The docs’ word for “maintenance loop.”
間mameaningful interval / negative spaceBoth meanings apply: the interval between runs, and the empty space of a healthy dashboard. One kanji, the whole product.
判hanthe seal / stampDispositions. To ack a finding is to stamp it. Hanko stamps as status iconography: 済 settled, 認 acknowledged, 休 snoozed — and 承 approved, the one stamp that becomes an order.
改善kaizencontinuous improvementThe philosophy of small recurring passes — the reason loops exist at all. Voice-of-brand word, not UI copy.
04 · Color · 色

Ink, paper, and one seal of vermillion.

Traditional Japanese color names (nihon no iro), each with a job. Vermillion is the only accent — it appears exactly where a hanko would: on things a human decided.

生成kinari · raw paper#F2EDE3The surface. Warm, faintly textured washi.

05 · The mark · 印

An ensō is a loop drawn with discipline.

The ensō (円相) — the zen circle, one breath, one stroke — is the rare logo concept that is not a metaphor. It literally is a loop. Left slightly open it is a run in progress; closed, a run complete.

Primary — the open ensō

Brush-weighted, imperfect, gap at the top right: the loop that will run again. The whole identity in one stroke.

巡

Seal — 巡 in vermillion

The hanko form for favicons, stamps, and small sizes. 巡: to make the rounds.

roops巡

Lockup — wordmark + seal

Lowercase serif wordmark, sealed like a signed document. The seal always sits last, as a signature does.
